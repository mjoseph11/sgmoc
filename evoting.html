The 18-hour registration window

To vote electronically you must first register, and registration opens and closes inside a single overnight window, Indian time. Miss it and you cannot vote. Wherever you are, that window lands on your own clock at a different hour — and in the Americas it closes on Wednesday night, a day earlier than most people assume.

Three ways to get the time wrong

Each of these catches people out on an ordinary conversion table.

In the Americas it closes Wednesday night

Pacific and Arizona shut at 10:30 PM Wednesday, Mountain at 11:30 PM Wednesday. Anyone planning to register “Thursday morning” will find it closed, by twelve hours.

Phoenix is not on Mountain time

Arizona does not observe daylight saving, so through August it runs on the Pacific clock — an hour behind the rest of the Mountain zone.

Atlantic Canada and Newfoundland

Halifax sits at UTC−3, and Newfoundland at UTC−2:30 — a half-hour offset that almost no conversion table bothers to include.

If your details are wrong on the list

The one-time password is sent to whatever mobile number and email address appear against your name on the official e-voting list. If either is out of date — or belongs to someone else — your voting code will go to the wrong place and you will not be able to vote. Check your entry now, and correct it before registration opens.
