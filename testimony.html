What I Have Seen

A personal testimony about Rev. Fr. Shibu Venad Mathai

By Mathew Joseph St. Gregorios Orthodox Church, Bensalem

I have been a member of St. Gregorios Orthodox Church in Bensalem since November 2017. Fr. Shibu Venad Mathai — Shibu Achen — has been our Vicar throughout that time.

There are many things that can be written about his education, his years of ministry, and the responsibilities he has held in the Church. Those are part of the public record.

I want to write about something different: what I have personally seen.

He is present when people need him

One of the things I came to understand very quickly about Shibu Achen is that being a priest is not something he does only on Sundays.

Whether someone is in the hospital, going through a family difficulty, needs prayer, or simply needs someone to listen, he makes himself available.

Our parish has more than 150 families. Yet I have rarely seen him make people feel that he is too busy for them.

During the fasts, he leads morning, noon and evening prayers. Saturday evening prayer has become part of the rhythm of parish life. He regularly invites Metropolitans and other clergy to speak so that our parish can learn from voices beyond our own.

After the previous episcopal election, he brought the newly elected bishops together to address our Great Lent convention over Zoom — every one of them, to a single parish, in the middle of the fast.

For many of us, his presence has simply become something we expect. That says a great deal about him.

During COVID, he kept the parish connected

The COVID period showed me another side of his ministry.

When our church could no longer gather normally, Shibu Achen made sure that worship did not disappear from our lives. Holy Qurbana continued to be celebrated and streamed so that families could participate from their homes.

At a time when almost everything around us was uncertain, the prayers of the Church remained familiar and constant.

What began out of necessity did not disappear when the pandemic ended. The services continue to be streamed today, allowing elderly parishioners, people who are sick, and people who cannot physically come to Bensalem to remain connected to the worship of the parish.

It is a small example of something I have seen repeatedly in him: when he recognizes a genuine pastoral need, he tries to find a practical way to meet it.

He listens before he speaks

Shibu Achen is not someone who needs to dominate a conversation.

When there is disagreement or tension, he listens. He has an ability to hear different sides without immediately judging one person right and another wrong. People who speak with him generally leave knowing that they were heard.

That quality may not attract much attention, but in the life of a parish it matters enormously. Leadership in the Church is not only about making decisions. Sometimes it is about creating enough trust that people can remain together even when they disagree.

I have watched him do that many times.

He prepares — and then lets others enjoy the occasion

Whenever our Diocesan Metropolitan, H.G. Zachariah Mar Nicholovos, visits Bensalem, a tremendous amount of preparation happens beforehand. The services, seating, meals, schedules and countless other details have already been considered.

As a result, the parish experiences the visit as a joyful occasion rather than a last-minute scramble.

That is typical of the way Shibu Achen works. Much of his leadership happens before anyone notices that leadership was required.

He thinks about the next generation

One decision particularly impressed me.

Shibu Achen recognized that ministering effectively to our young people required dedicated attention. With the approval of our Diocesan Metropolitan, he helped establish an Associate Minister role and brought Mrs. Ceena Mariam Mathew, who holds a Master of Arts and a Master of Theology from St. Vladimir’s Orthodox Theological Seminary, into that ministry.

The significance for me was not simply the appointment itself. It was the thinking behind it.

He understood that preserving our traditions is essential — but that preserving the Church also means finding ways to keep the next generation connected to those traditions.

He saw a need and was willing to pursue a new approach to address it. That combination of respect for tradition and willingness to respond to changing pastoral needs is something I value greatly in him.

His ministry extends beyond Sunday worship

During his years at Bensalem, the parish has developed ministries serving young adults, families, elderly members and the wider community.

The Ministry and Mission Center was established. The Good Samaritan Mission began serving elderly and isolated people. Ministries such as Logos have focused on younger members of the Church.

He has also given his time to Building a Better Bensalem Together, the township’s volunteer coalition working to keep young people away from substance abuse, and has developed relationships with Christians outside our own Malankara Orthodox community.

To me, this reflects a simple understanding of ministry: the responsibility of a priest does not end at the church property line.

I have also seen his personal simplicity

There are qualities about a priest that do not appear in reports or official biographies. One of them is his attitude toward money and recognition.

I have personally seen Shibu Achen decline offerings made to him for pastoral visits and blessings. Acts of service are not something he treats as transactions.

What money does come to him, he largely gives away. A good deal of it goes to families in need in India. He does not speak about this, and I am conscious that he would rather I had not written it down.

Nor is he someone who constantly reminds people what he has done. Many of the things he does become known only because someone else happens to mention them.

And then there is something I will never forget

For me, all of these observations became deeply personal when my mother, Ramani Joseph, passed away.

Shibu Achen was in India at the time. Our family was in Ernakulam.

He did not have to come. It was not his parish there, and nobody would have questioned him if he had simply called us and offered his prayers.

But he came to our home. And he conducted the first funeral service for my mother.

There are moments when you discover what someone’s ministry really means to them. For me, that was one of those moments.

I will never forget it.

Why I am writing this now

On 27 August 2026 the Malankara Association elected Shibu Achen to the episcopacy, one of the four chosen.

A great many people across the Church now know his name. Far fewer know the man.

St. Gregorios Orthodox Church is also celebrating its Golden Jubilee this year. Shibu Achen has served as its Vicar since June 2011 — fifteen years of the parish’s fifty-year history.

Fifteen years is long enough to see someone through ordinary Sundays and extraordinary circumstances, through disagreements and celebrations, through births, illnesses and deaths.

What I have seen during my years with him is remarkably consistent.

He is available. He listens. He prepares. He cares about the next generation. He serves without making service about himself. And when his people need their priest, he comes.

Those qualities are difficult to capture in a résumé. But they are the qualities by which I have come to know Shibu Achen.

The wider Church is about to receive him. Those who have not met him deserve to know what kind of priest he has been for fifteen years, in one parish, when nobody outside it was watching.

That is why I wanted to set down what I have seen.

Mathew Joseph
St. Gregorios Orthodox Church · Bensalem, Pennsylvania
