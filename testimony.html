St. Gregorios Orthodox Church · Bensalem, Pennsylvania

A Parishioner’s Testimony

Rev. Fr. Shibu Venad Mathai

By Mathew Joseph — a member of the parish since November 2017

Shibu Achen has been our Vicar since 2011. What I want to set down here is not praise but what I have actually seen, because with him the ordinary things say more than the remarkable ones.

Through COVID, he kept us fed

When the parish could not gather, Achen made certain it was not left without worship. The Holy Qurbana continued at Bensalem week after week — Achen, and whichever of our kappiyars was taking his turn — and he had every service streamed so that families could pray along from their own homes. There is no assistant vicar at Bensalem; there never has been.

Those were lonely months for most of us. What he gave the parish was continuity: the same prayers, the same voice, at the same hour, when very little else was steady. He never spoke of it as a sacrifice.

That stream has never stopped. The liturgy still goes out from Bensalem every Sunday, years after the doors reopened — and because it does, the housebound, the unwell and those far from Bensalem are still part of the congregation.

He is always available

To the church and to its people, without qualification. Every Saturday evening there is prayer. Through the fasts he conducts morning, noon and evening prayer, and in the evenings he often adds a convention, bringing in Metropolitans and other distinguished clergy so the parish hears voices beyond his own. If you need him, he is there — at the hospital, at the house, on the phone. In nine years I have not known him to be unreachable. That constancy is the thing the parish leans on without noticing, the way you do not notice a floor until it gives.

He brings calm

There is a steadiness about Achen that settles a room. He does not take sides and he does not lecture. He listens for longer than is comfortable, and people find they have been properly heard — which is rarer than it sounds, and does more good than most advice. Whatever is difficult tends to become easier in his presence, and afterwards nobody can quite say how. It is a gift, and he uses it so lightly that you can miss it happening.

Nothing is left to chance

When H. G. Zachariah Mar Nicholovos, our Diocesan Metropolitan, visits Bensalem, the remark afterwards is always the same: that everything was organised, and there was no confusion at all. Thirumeni is received the same way every time. The services, the seating, the meal, the people who should be presented to him — all of it worked out before anyone arrives, so that the day runs without a raised voice or a hurried decision.

That is not luck. It is a man who thinks the details through in advance so that nobody else has to, and so that a Metropolitan’s visit is a joy to the parish rather than a strain on it.

Fifty years, and fifteen of them his

Our parish was founded in 1976. This year it keeps its Golden Jubilee, and Achen has led it since June 2011 — the successor to its founding vicar, and the second priest in its history. St. Gregorios, Bensalem is among the larger Malankara Orthodox parishes in the United States, more than 150 families, and for fifteen years it has had one priest and no assistant.

What has been built in those years is on the record. The Ministry and Mission Center was dedicated in 2016. The Good Samaritan Mission was founded the same year and now has more than twenty-five members visiting the elderly and the isolated. The Logos Ministry was launched for young adults. He is spiritual director to all of it — Sunday School, the Youth League, Logos, GROW, St. Joseph’s Forum, the Good Samaritan Mission — and he led the Jubilee itself.

He saw something before the rest of us

Achen went to Nicholovos Thirumeni and sought his permission to appoint Mrs. Ceena Mariam Mathew as Associate Minister — an appointment no one in our Church had made before.

His reason was the young people. He had been saying for years that a parish which loses its youth has lost its future, and he judged that ours needed someone who could give them their whole attention rather than what was left over at the end of a priest’s week.

Ceena graduated from St. Vladimir’s Orthodox Theological Seminary with a Master of Arts and a Master of Theology, and has taught at seminary herself. The learning was never in question. What was missing was a priest willing to ask whether it could be put to use in a parish.

It has borne fruit far beyond Bensalem. She gave the Lenten message at a New Jersey parish in 2021. In October 2023 she was keynote speaker at the diocesan Marth Mariam Vanitha Samajam conference in Philadelphia, with Thirumeni in the chair and priests and deacons in the hall. She has twice given the devotional message at the diocese’s Friday fasting prayer; one of those is on YouTube, so anyone who wonders whether the appointment was justified can go and listen.

It would have been easier not to ask. He asked, and Thirumeni gave it. He did not wait to be told it was possible.

He will not take money

If you try to give him money, he refuses. When he comes to bless a new car, he will not accept the offering. What does reach him he sends to families in need in India, and he says nothing of it to anyone. I write this knowing he would rather I did not.

He came

When my mother, Ramani Joseph, passed away, Achen was in India. He came to our home in Ernakulam and conducted the first funeral service.

He was not our priest there. It was not his parish, not his duty, and nobody would have thought less of him had he sent word instead. He came. I have not forgotten it, and I will not.

Beyond the parish

He is active with Building a Better Bensalem Together — B3T, the township’s volunteer coalition working to keep young people away from drugs and the trouble that follows them. It is not a church body. It is neighbours of every background and none, working on something the whole township needs, and Achen gives his time to it for children who will never sit in his church.

He has also built a real working relationship with the Coptic Orthodox Church here — the kind of quiet ecumenism that happens between neighbours rather than at conferences.

The priesthood is his only occupation. He holds no secular employment and never has. His time has belonged to the parish and to whatever the Diocese has asked of him — the Holy Transfiguration Retreat Centre, the Diocesan Council, teaching for Divyabodhanam.

I have set these things down plainly, because Achen would be embarrassed by anything warmer, and because they are enough on their own.

Mathew Joseph
St. Gregorios Orthodox Church, Bensalem
